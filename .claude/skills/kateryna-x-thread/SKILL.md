# Kateryna on X

Write threads that read like they were born on X, not like a LinkedIn post chopped into pieces.

This builds on `kateryna-voice`. Everything there still holds: her tics ("blah blah", "bc", "etc.", "here we are"), proof by real specifics, buzzword-disarm, honest self-deprecating asides, and every hard ban (no em/en dashes, no "not X it's Y", no AI slop, and especially no tidy symmetric mic-drop pairs like "I came in knowing how to work hard. I left knowing how to build.").

The voice is the same. What changes is the format.

## Same content rule as the voice skill

Voice and format are reusable. Content is not. Never drag the facts or one-off lines from one post into another (the Apify build, the terminal line, Sam Altman, "here we are" as a crutch). Each thread brings its own real specifics. If you need a proof point and don't have a real one, leave a `[bracket placeholder]` and ask. Never invent or borrow one.

## What makes it X and not LinkedIn

### 1. Lowercase
Default to all lowercase. It reads casual and native. Capitalize only proper nouns where it would look wrong not to (SF, YC, names), and even those can stay lower if the vibe calls for it.

### 2. The hook tweet front-loads the most surprising fact
Tweet 1 leads with the single most interesting or unexpected line, not a slow opener. On LinkedIn she opens "I left San Francisco." On X the same story opens with "six months ago i didn't know how to open a terminal." Lead with the thing that makes someone stop scrolling, then a one-line promise of what the thread delivers.

A good hook tweet is usually 3 short lines with white space between them:
> six months ago i didn't know how to open a terminal
>
> now i build for a living
>
> what 6 months in SF actually did to me 🧵

### 3. Lists instead of long sentences
On LinkedIn her wins run as one comma sentence. On X, break them into a scannable list:
> in that time i:
>
> - won a GTM hackathon
> - built a product that won best use of apify
> - did a YC hackathon
> - met sam altman

### 4. White space and line breaks inside each tweet
Break lines for thumb-scrolling. One thought per line. Let tweets breathe instead of packing a paragraph in.

### 5. Loose punctuation
Drop trailing periods on short lines. Casual is the point. Don't make it grammatically tidy.

### 6. Keep her voice in the casual translation
Her LinkedIn filler becomes X filler. "Everyone throws this buzzword around" becomes "i know, buzzword." "Let me know" becomes "lmk." Keep "here we are", keep "bc", keep the honest asides.

### 7. Still no engineered staccato
Short lines on X are fine, but do not fall into the "short. short. short. mic drop." metronome the writing-skill bans. Vary line length. Let a longer messy line run when the thought needs it. Human, not drum machine.

## Mechanics

- Each tweet must be under 280 characters. Count before delivering.
- Number tweets 1/, 2/, etc. so she can see the structure.
- Emoji: minimal. A 🧵 on the hook and a 👇 on the last tweet is plenty, and even those are optional. Her writing otherwise has no emoji, so never sprinkle them mid-thread.
- End by opening a door, not with a motivational bow: "more soon. lmk if you want more X lessons 👇"
