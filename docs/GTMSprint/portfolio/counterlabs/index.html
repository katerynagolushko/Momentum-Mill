CounterLabs

Turns a shop's existing CCTV into a model of how people move and buy, without identifying them.

counterlabs.ai

The company

CounterLabs starts with the cameras a shop already has and turns that footage into a picture of how people move: where they linger, what they pick up, and how a visit becomes a sale. The video is processed on a device in the store. The retailer sees movement, not faces, names or individual profiles.

Sam Coyne is the founder.

The week

He started in stealth. No customers, no revenue, no pilots, a generic landing page, and no demo he could put in front of anyone. The target he wrote on day one was 50 people contacted and 5 follow-up conversations.

The first job was to have something to show. He came out of stealth, put up a new landing page, finished a working demo, and set up a work email and a phone number. He published a first LinkedIn post, filmed on the high street in Camden with Misky Sharif, and talked through next steps with his mentor, Noel Coenraad. Then he walked into more than 30 shops.

He had a proper conversation with 20 store managers. He is arranging 11 follow-up meetings, and he was given 3 CEO email addresses to write to. He also had a chat with the CTO of Sainsbury's. CounterLabs is now the top Google result for its own name, a post of his on Google had 1,400 impressions, and he was invited to his local chamber of retail committee.

CounterLabs won Emerging Ventures at Demo Day.
